## What this is

A fictional client brief for a property called **Cedar & Stone Inn**. Use it whenever you need something to practice against — a hero, a rooms grid, a meta block, a Things-to-Do page, a copy pass. The brief is detailed enough to feel real, generic enough that you can't accidentally cargo-cult someone's actual website.

Don't try to "build the whole site." Pick one section, build it well, move on.

---

## The property

**Cedar & Stone Inn** is a six-room bed & breakfast in a fictional Hudson Valley town called **Cedar Valley, NY**. Built in 1903 as a private farmhouse, converted to an inn in 2008 by a couple — **Maggie and Tom Whitford** — who used to run a restaurant in Brooklyn and decided they wanted slower mornings.

The inn sits on four acres at the edge of a forest preserve. There's a stone fireplace in the common room, a wraparound porch where breakfast is served on warm mornings, and a small kitchen garden that supplies most of what shows up on the table. Maggie cooks. Tom handles bookings, the wood, and the dog (a quiet retriever named Wren).

The aesthetic: linen, weathered wood, soft lamps, real food. Not "rustic-luxury." Just lived-in.

---

## The audience

- **Couples**, mostly 35–60. Weekend travelers from NYC, Boston, Philadelphia.
- **Foodies** — the breakfast is the draw, and there's good local food in the area (a small farmer's market Saturdays, two cideries, a respected farm-to-table restaurant 12 minutes away).
- **Hikers and slow-trip people** — there's a trailhead three minutes' walk from the inn that connects into the forest preserve.
- Occasional **dog-friendly** travelers (two of the six rooms allow dogs).
- Not a wedding venue. Not a corporate retreat. Not a kid-focused destination (though kids over 10 are welcome with notice).

---

## The voice

- Warm, specific, observational.
- Low-key. Doesn't oversell.
- Names things. "Tuesday-morning sourdough," "Wren the retriever," "the porch swing that was here before we were."
- Avoids hospitality clichés ("luxurious," "stunning," "world-class," "unforgettable experience," "destination").
- First-person plural is fine when Maggie and Tom are speaking. Otherwise default to second-person ("you") or descriptive third-person.
- Long-form is okay where it earns it (a paragraph about breakfast). Short and quiet where it doesn't.

---

## Brand cues

- **Palette**: warm cream, deep moss green, walnut brown, soft black. No bright accent.
- **Typography**: serif display font for headlines (something in the Cormorant or Tiempos family), clean sans for body (Inter, Söhne, or similar).
- **Photography**: morning light, soft contrast, food up close, hands and details. Not staged "lifestyle" shots with people pretending to laugh.
- **Logo**: wordmark in serif, lowercase. No icon, no monogram.

---

## Must-haves

- Direct booking through ResNexus (the engine they currently use).
- A "rooms" page with all six rooms.
- A "things to do" page that links to local spots (the cideries, the trail, the farmer's market, the respected restaurant).
- An innkeeper letter — Maggie's voice — on the home page or About page.
- Mobile-first. The majority of bookings come through phones.
- ADA-accessible at the page level (heading hierarchy, alt text, keyboard navigation).

## Constraints

- Photography budget for the rebuild: small. There's an existing library of about 80 usable photos taken over five years. New shoot might happen in spring; this build can't wait for it.
- No video.
- The inn doesn't have an Instagram presence — Maggie isn't into social. Don't build the site assuming an Instagram feed widget at the bottom.
- Two rooms allow dogs; four don't. The website needs to make this clear without being clinical about it.
- Maggie writes most of the copy herself. It's good. Don't rewrite — polish.

---

## Deliverable for practice

Pick one of these to build for a 30–45 minute exercise:

- The home page hero with the welcome card just below it.
- The rooms grid (six cards) with one room's single page.
- The "things to do" page with the local-area map block, four nearby spots, and a CTA banner at the bottom.
- The contact page with a form, the inn's address, hours, and a small "what to expect when you arrive" note.
- The home-page footer including the press strip and newsletter signup.

For copy: write it as if Maggie wrote it. Specific, warm, no clichés. If you find yourself reaching for "stunning" or "perfect getaway," start over.

For images: use placeholders from Unsplash or a local stock library. Pick photos that match the brand cues above (morning light, soft contrast, food close-up, no staged people). Note in your handoff which photos are placeholders.

When you're done, run the work through `rubric.mdx` before showing it to anyone.
