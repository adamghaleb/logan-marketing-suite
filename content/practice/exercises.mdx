## How to use these

Six exercises, ten to forty minutes each. Real-shaped prompts — they look like the kind of small task a PM would actually drop on you mid-week. Done is better than perfect. Perfect is the wrong target anyway.

Pick one. Set a timer. Build it against the Cedar & Stone Inn brief in `sample-brief.mdx`. Run the result through `rubric.mdx`. Move on.

If you want to do them in order, the difficulty curves up. If you want to mix, that's fine — they're independent.

---

## 1. Build a hero with one line of copy

**Time**: 20 minutes.

**The task**: Build the Cedar & Stone home page hero. One photo, one line of copy, no button. The line should be specific and quiet — not a slogan, not a promise.

**Inputs**:

- Use a placeholder Unsplash photo of a porch or kitchen with morning light.
- Write the headline yourself. One line. Maximum 8 words.

**Success looks like**:

- The headline is in the Logan style — specific, sensory, no clichés.
- The photo's focal point survives a mobile crop.
- The headline color picks up something already in the photo, not a brand-red dropped on top.
- No black overlay was added to "fix" contrast.

**Common pitfall**: writing a slogan ("Your unforgettable Hudson Valley escape"). That's not the brand. Try again.

## 2. Write a welcome card from a fact list

**Time**: 25 minutes.

**The task**: Write the welcome card paragraph from the innkeepers (Maggie, with Tom's name in there too) using only the facts below. Three to four sentences. Sign it with a placeholder signature.

**Facts to use** (pick the three that feel most alive — don't use them all):

- Used to run a restaurant in Brooklyn, moved upstate in 2008.
- Maggie cooks breakfast. Tom handles wood, bookings, and Wren the dog.
- Six rooms. Four acres. A trailhead three minutes' walk from the porch.
- They keep a kitchen garden. Most of what shows up at breakfast came from it that week.
- Wren is a quiet retriever, ten years old, sleeps under the breakfast table.
- The porch is where you'll spend most of your time, weather permitting.

**Success looks like**:

- It sounds like Maggie wrote it. Not like a real estate listing. Not like a brochure.
- One specific detail you can picture (the dog under the table, the kitchen garden, the morning light on the porch).
- No "We pride ourselves on…" or "Our passion is…"
- Three to four sentences.

**Common pitfall**: trying to fit all six facts in. The card is short. Pick three.

## 3. Build a rooms grid (3 cards)

**Time**: 30 minutes.

**The task**: Build the rooms landing page with three cards (representing three of the six rooms). Photo, name, two-line body, optional rate in mono.

**Inputs**:

- Make up three room names that fit the brand. ("The Garden Room" works; "Suite 3" doesn't.)
- Write two lines of body for each. Specific.
- Use placeholder photos. Same crop ratio across all three.

**Success looks like**:

- Cards are identical in spacing and structure (because you templated one and reused).
- All three photos are the same crop ratio.
- Body copy is two lines on desktop. Doesn't wrap to four.
- Mobile stacks to one column.
- The whole tile is the link — no separate "Book Now" button on the card.

**Common pitfall**: building three cards by hand without templating. Spacing will drift. Template the first one.

## 4. Fix a misaligned mobile section (debug exercise)

**Time**: 15 minutes.

**The task**: Take a working desktop section (any of the ones from exercises 1–3) and switch to mobile view. Find what's broken. Fix it. Document what you found in 3 sentences.

**Inputs**:

- Use one of your earlier exercise builds.
- Look at it on a real phone if you can.

**Success looks like**:

- You correctly identify which of the four common problems it was (direction, padding, image, breakpoint inheritance).
- You fix it without adding hacks (no `display:none`, no negative margin, no overlay).
- You can describe the fix in plain English to a non-technical person.

**Common pitfall**: fixing the symptom by hiding the broken element. That's not fixing — that's hiding. If something is broken, debug the cause.

## 5. Write a meta title and description for one page

**Time**: 10 minutes.

**The task**: Write meta title and meta description for the Cedar & Stone "Things to do" page. Pick a focus keyword. Set them in Yoast or Rank Math (or write them in a doc if you don't have a WordPress install handy).

**Inputs**:

- Property: Cedar & Stone Inn, Cedar Valley, NY.
- Page topic: things to do near the inn.
- Real-feeling local spots to mention: a trail, a cidery, a farmer's market.

**Success looks like**:

- Meta title is 50–60 characters.
- Meta description is 140–160 characters and uses one specific local detail.
- Focus keyword is one phrase, not five.
- The description doesn't start with "Welcome to…" or "Discover…"

**Common pitfall**: using the same description across multiple pages. Each one gets its own.

## 6. Polish a paragraph of client copy

**Time**: 25 minutes.

**The task**: Maggie sent over a paragraph for the Things-to-Do page. It's mostly good, but it has two clichés and one sentence that's a little long. Polish it. Keep her voice — don't rewrite, don't shorten just to shorten. Send back a clean version with two or three flagged questions for her.

**The paragraph**:

> "There are so many wonderful things to do in our region. Whether you're looking for a romantic getaway or an unforgettable adventure, the Hudson Valley has it all — from world-class wineries to charming small towns, you'll find something to love. We recommend starting with a hike on the Cedar Ridge Trail (it's right out our front door, basically), then heading to Whitfield Cider for a tasting, and finishing with dinner at The Brookhouse, which is genuinely the best restaurant within thirty miles. Don't miss the Saturday farmer's market in town — it's small but very good."

**Success looks like**:

- "World-class," "unforgettable," "you'll find something to love," and "has it all" are gone.
- Maggie's voice (the parenthetical "(it's right out our front door, basically)") is preserved.
- The paragraph is a touch shorter, not because you cut content but because you cut filler.
- You've flagged 2–3 things to ask Maggie about — maybe the exact name of the trail, whether "thirty miles" is accurate, whether she wants to mention the cidery's outdoor seating.

**Common pitfall**: rewriting the whole paragraph in your own voice. The job is polish, not rewrite. If your version sounds nothing like the original, you've gone too far.
