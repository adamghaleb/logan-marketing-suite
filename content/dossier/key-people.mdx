> **Heads up: these are not real names.** I (Adam) sketched them as archetypes — the kinds of leads you tend to find inside a 40-person brand-led shop. The goal is that no role in the room surprises you, not that you can name a single person. When they introduce themselves, just listen for the **shape**: which archetype they map to, which they don't.

A shop Logan's size usually has a small founding bench (one or two leads with their names on the door, sometimes literally), a strategy lead, a creative lead, a growth lead, and a few senior individual contributors who anchor the practice. You will probably meet two to four people in the interview process. Here's the cast you might see, drawn as composites.

## The founder / managing partner

**Likely role in the conversation:** opens or closes. Sets the temperature for the whole interview. Asks the broadest, most open-ended questions ("what kind of work do you want to do in five years?"), and the answers they care about are the un-rehearsed ones.

**What they probably look for:**

- Real opinions, lightly held.
- Specific stories with one concrete detail that proves you were actually there.
- A sense that you're choosing them, not just collecting offers.

**What probably bores them:**

- Frameworks recited at length.
- Job-description language ("I'm a results-oriented self-starter").
- Anything that sounds like you'd give the same answer at any agency.

**How to land it:** answer their first question with a 30-second story, not a thesis. Let them ask the second question. The interview is a conversation, not a presentation.

## The strategy lead

**Likely role:** asks about audience, positioning, and how you'd approach an ambiguous brief. Hands you a half-formed prompt and watches you organize it.

**What they probably look for:**

- The instinct to ask "who is this for?" before "what should we make?"
- Comfort with not knowing yet. They don't want quick answers; they want clean questions.
- The ability to disagree with a brief without performing disagreement.

**Possible warm-up question:** "Walk me through how you'd open up a project you don't know much about."

**How to land it:** narrate the questions you'd ask before the work you'd do. The shape of your thinking is the work, here.

## The creative lead

**Likely role:** looks at your portfolio. Asks about specific decisions, not the whole project. The question after "I love that piece" is the real one — usually something like "why is the type that small?"

**What they probably look for:**

- That you can defend a small choice.
- That you can say "I don't remember why" without panicking.
- That you have taste, and that you can articulate it without sounding precious.

**How to land it:** for each piece you walk through, have one sentence about the decision you'd defend hardest and one sentence about something you'd do differently now. Both. Not just the win.

## The growth lead

**Likely role:** asks how you think about measurement, channels, and the day-to-day cadence of a brand that's also trying to grow. Probably the most operational person in the room.

**What they probably look for:**

- That you don't roll your eyes at metrics.
- That you don't roll your eyes at brand, either.
- A working theory of how brand work and growth work feed each other instead of fighting.

**How to land it:** if you don't have deep growth experience, say so plainly, and then say what you'd want to learn. "I haven't owned a paid budget. I'd want to sit close to whoever does for the first quarter and ask annoying questions" is a great answer.

## The senior individual contributor (writer, designer, or strategist)

**Likely role:** appears in a working session or a portfolio walkthrough. Less interested in your résumé, more interested in what it's like to actually work next to you.

**What they probably look for:**

- Curiosity without insecurity.
- Whether you make things easier or harder for the person sitting next to you.
- Whether you can take feedback without absorbing it as identity.

**How to land it:** ask them a real question about how they work. "What's a project where the brief landed wrong and you had to push back?" is the kind of thing a peer asks a peer.

## A note on titles

Don't get hung up on titles in the moment. A small shop's "VP of Strategy" might do the same day-to-day as another shop's "Senior Strategist." Listen for what someone actually does, not what their email signature says.

## The one universal move

For every person in the room, near the end of the conversation, ask them this: **"What would the first 30 days here actually look like for me?"**

It's a generous question. It signals you're imagining the job, not just trying to get it. And every interviewer answers it slightly differently, which gives you a lot of triangulated truth about how the place really runs.
