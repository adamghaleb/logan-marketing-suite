> **Heads up: every client name on this page is invented.** I (Adam) couldn't get a verified list of Logan's full book, so this is a composite — fictional brands shaped to match the kinds of accounts a shop their size and shape would credibly run. The point isn't to memorize names. It's to walk in with a clear mental picture of the **kinds** of brands they work with, so when they mention a real one, you have a frame ready.

Logan's book most likely sits across three clusters: mid-market direct-to-consumer, boutique hospitality, and lifestyle brands with a real point of view. Roughly 25–30 active accounts at any time, with the mix tilted slightly toward DTC by revenue and slightly toward hospitality by volume.

## Direct-to-consumer (composite)

Mid-market brands with a real product, a small internal team, and a CEO who reads the work. The kind of account where Logan can do strategy, creative, and growth together without three other vendors in the room.

| Brand (fictional)      | What they make                             | Why Logan would fit                                                  |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Foundry & Field        | Cast-iron cookware, single-product line    | Founder-led, photo-driven, needs an audience-first brand voice       |
| North Honey Co.        | Single-origin honey, gift-forward          | Long-form storytelling, sensory writing, gifting moments             |
| Maple & Ash Apothecary | Botanical skincare, slow-grown ingredients | Editorial product pages, wellness without hype                       |
| Field Notes Coffee     | Subscription roaster, regional sourcing    | Content calendar, lifecycle email, zero performance-mercenary energy |

Common thread: brands that already know who they are, and need someone who won't accidentally flatten them.

## Boutique hospitality (composite)

Inns, boutique hotels, ranches, and small resort properties. Often family-owned, often outside major cities, often with a real story and real photography. This cluster is large enough that Logan has a repeatable production system for it (Elementor sites, brand kit, booking integration, local SEO, gallery patterns).

| Property (fictional)     | Type                   | Where                 |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------- | --------------------- |
| The Sycamore Inn         | 12-room boutique inn   | Hudson Valley         |

Common thread: place-rooted, story-rich, photographable. The visual system is doing a lot of the work, and the copy needs to match.

## Lifestyle (composite)

Brands that aren't strictly DTC and aren't strictly hospitality but live in the same audience neighborhood: home, food, slow living, design-forward. Often editorial or content-led.

| Brand (fictional) | What they are                                    |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Common Table      | A cookbook + kitchenware imprint                 |
| Quiet Hours       | A weekend-trip newsletter that grew into a brand |
| Earnest Goods     | A small-town general store that ships nationally |

Common thread: the editorial muscle is already there; Logan amplifies it without changing the voice.

## What the mix tells you

A few things to internalize from the shape of the book, regardless of whether the specific names above are right.

- **They like brands that have a soul already.** Logan sharpens; they don't manufacture.
- **The work spans channels.** Brand voice has to survive a homepage, a print piece, a 90-word Instagram caption, and a lifecycle email.
- **Hospitality is its own muscle.** If they ask about it, lean in — it's a real strength of the shop and the [agency profile](/dossier/agency-profile) backs that up.
- **They probably avoid pure-performance shops.** Don't pitch yourself as a paid-media person, even if you have those chops. Lead with brand and audience.

## How to bring this up in the room

Don't list these names. They're not real, and trying to recall a fake brand mid-sentence is the worst possible outcome.

Instead: when they mention a real client, listen for the **shape** — DTC product brand, boutique inn, lifestyle imprint — and respond with the right register. "That kind of account is fun to work on because the audience already trusts the brand; the job is mostly to not get in the way." That sentence works for any of the above.
