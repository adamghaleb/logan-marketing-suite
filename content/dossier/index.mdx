This is what I (Adam) could find on Logan Marketing before you walked in. Some of it is fact — the public-facing positioning, the kinds of clients they show off on the site. A lot of it is composite: educated guesses based on the industry, the kind of work they put out, and what a 40-ish-person brand-led shop usually looks like inside.

Read it as a primer, not a script. The goal is that nothing in the room surprises you, not that you can recite their org chart. If a question comes up that this dossier doesn't answer, that's fine — say "I don't know yet, but here's how I'd find out," and move on.

Composite/inferred content is flagged in every file. When you see "this is a guess," it means I extrapolated. When you see "this is from their site," it means I read it.

## What's in here

- **Agency profile** — what Logan does, roughly how big, where they sit in the market.
- **Voice analysis** — how they write, with do/don't pairs you can mirror in the interview.
- **Client list** — the kinds of brands they likely work with. Composite, plausible, not real names.
- **Key people** — the founders and leads you might meet, sketched as archetypes.
- **First 90 days** — your draft 30/60/90 plan template. You'll fill in real specifics after the conversation.
- **Questions for them** — five prepared questions: two strategic, two team/culture, one weird-but-good.

## How to use it

Skim the whole thing once the night before. Read **voice analysis** and **questions for them** twice. Print the **first 90 days** page if it helps you think — it's the one that makes you sound like you've already started the job in your head.

Don't memorize. The version of you they want in the room is curious and observant, not over-prepared. If you walk in having absorbed the shape of this rather than the words, that's exactly right.
