Logan Marketing is a brand-led growth shop for category-defining consumer companies. They sit in the middle of the market: bigger than a freelancer collective, smaller than a holding-company agency, the size where the founders still read the work before it ships. Roughly 40 people across strategy, creative, and growth. About 25–30 active accounts at any given time.

Their bread and butter is mid-market direct-to-consumer, hospitality, and lifestyle brands — the kind of company with a real product and a real audience but not yet a real internal marketing team. Logan does the strategy, the brand work, the campaigns, and a lot of the always-on growth. Hospitality (inns, boutique hotels, ranches, B&Bs) is a notable cluster — strong enough that they have a recognizable production system for it.

If you want a one-line frame: **they're the agency a growing brand hires when they've outgrown a freelancer but aren't ready to staff up internally.**

## Where they sit in the market

| Dimension         | Logan                                                            |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Size              | ~40 people                                                       |
| Active accounts   | ~25–30                                                           |
| Verticals         | Mid-market DTC, hospitality, lifestyle                           |
| Style             | Brand-led, audience-first, long-form-friendly                    |
| Where they're not | Performance-only shops, enterprise-scale RFPs, pure media buyers |

The specific differentiator (this is observation, not corporate speak) is that they lead with audience and brand, then let channel choice fall out of that. Most shops their size do it the other way — channel first, brand voice as a coat of paint. Logan flips it.

## How a project usually goes

This is a composite based on how shops their shape and size tend to work. Verify in the room.

1. **Strategy** opens the engagement: audience work, positioning, a brief.
2. **Creative** builds the brand expression — campaigns, art direction, copy.
3. **Growth** runs the always-on side — content calendar, paid, lifecycle, measurement.
4. The three teams stay close on every account so the brand voice doesn't drift between them.

A junior coming in (your role) sits inside one of those legs and learns the shop's language for a few months before stretching across them.

## What they probably value in a hire

Drawn from how they write about themselves and how their work reads. Not from a job description.

- Strategists who lead with audience insight, not channel mechanics.
- Crisp writers. Fewer slides, sharper sentences.
- Strong opinions, lightly held — they want people who will push back, not posture.
- Pattern recognition over checklists. Junior hires who can spot when a brief is mid before they've finished reading it.

## What this means for you

You don't need to walk in with five years of agency experience. You need to walk in sounding like the kind of person who already thinks the way they think — audience first, voice tight, opinions earned.

The first 90 days plan in this dossier is a way to demonstrate that without saying "I think the way you think." Lead with it; don't announce it.
